A REMINDER

Videos are the spark.
Your experiments are the fire.

Pick a build idea →
01

Let the overview do its job.

A quick explainer is a map, not the whole territory. Use it to orient yourself, learn the vocabulary, and spot the part that makes you curious enough to go deeper.

LEARN
02

Pause at the “wait, how?” moment.

That tiny flash of confusion is useful. Write down the question before the next video answers it for you. Then see if you can answer it with docs, a small experiment, or both.

WONDER
03

Build the smallest possible version.

Heard about a new tool? Make it do one tiny thing. A hello-world project often teaches more than another hour of watching—and gives you something real to break and fix.

MAKE
04

Keep old explanations in perspective.

Software changes quickly. A video can be a useful snapshot, while the official documentation tells you what applies today. Check dates, version numbers, and release notes as you go.

CHECK
05

Share the thing you learned.

Explain the concept to a friend, leave a note for your future self, or publish the little project. Teaching it back is a satisfying way to find the gaps and celebrate how far you’ve come.
